Three guesses about voice in India.

I spend my days teaching people to put the right information into software. The hardest part usually isn't the software, it's the typing. Here's how I'd start thinking about Wispr Flow in India, written as questions to test rather than answers.

1.

Win on code-switching

I speak Tamil and Telugu natively, work in English, and my Hindi is limited. I switch mid-sentence all day. Dictation that copes with that could feel like magic.

My guess
Fluent mixed-language dictation is the sharpest wedge for Indian professionals who think in two languages but type in one.
How I'd test it
Record voice notes from ~20 colleagues and partners (starting with my own), compare how they'd say things with how they type them, and note the gaps.
What I don't know
Current language coverage and accuracy in the product. I'd need to try it first.
2.

Grow through ecosystems

At Zoho I help 150+ partners learn, sell and implement a product. The pattern, trusted local experts who demo a tool to their own clients, could carry over to voice productivity.

My guess
A small cohort of consultants, agencies and trainers who use voice daily is a more credible channel in India than broad ads.
How I'd test it
Run a 4-week pilot with a handful of agency owners: an onboarding kit, a weekly workflow tip, and see who keeps using it unprompted.
What I don't know
Pricing sensitivity, and whether a partner-style programme fits how the team already grows.
3.

Voice for the messy middle of work

Sales and support teams under-log calls because typing notes is a chore. Speaking a recap right after a call is faster, and the notes come out richer.

My guess
Small-business teams on CRMs are a natural early audience: the habit repeats daily and the payoff is visible.
How I'd test it
Ask 10 CRM users to dictate call notes for two weeks. Compare note length, completeness and time spent.
What I don't know
How this fits integrations and privacy expectations in different industries.

These are my own ideas, not built or validated products, and I'm not affiliated with Wispr Flow. I'm applying because I'd love to find out which of them survive contact with real users.
